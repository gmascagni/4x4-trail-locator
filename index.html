OVERLANDING EXPEDITION & CAMP AUTONOMY GUIDE
Fuel range calculations, potable water autonomy, dispersed camping & rooftop tent rules

Fuel Range Autonomy

4WD low-range and soft sand can cut fuel economy by 50%. Always carry RotopaX or Jerry cans to guarantee at least 250-300 miles of self-contained range before the next remote fuel stop.

Water & Filtration

Carry a minimum of 1 gallon per person per day plus 1 extra day emergency buffer. Equip a high-flow gravity water filter for creek and river refills.

Dispersed RTT Camping

Most BLM and National Forest land allows free dispersed camping up to 14 days. Level your vehicle with traction boards for comfortable Rooftop Tent sleep.

Satellite Comms & Nav

Cell coverage is non-existent in deep canyons and wilderness corridors. Download offline Gaia/OnX topo maps and carry a Garmin InReach satellite SOS beacon.

Overlanding Autonomy: Pro Tips & Trail Tricks

Expedition Survival Rules
1. The "Rule of Thirds" Fuel Strategy
In 4WD Low or deep clay/sand, your MPG drops by up to 50%. Plan your fuel in thirds: 1/3 to reach camp, 1/3 to return, and 1/3 in reserve for detours or trail blockages.
2. Leveling RTTs with Traction Boards
Never sleep on a tilt. Instead of searching for rocks or digging holes, drive your downhill tires onto your traction recovery boards to instantly level your Rooftop Tent anywhere.
3. 12V Dual-Zone Fridge Power Hack
Pre-chill your 12V compressor fridge on household 110V wall power the night before departure. Pack frozen food items solid—your fridge consumes 60% less battery maintaining temp.
4. Offline Map Pre-Caching
Download a 50-mile radius of USGS Topo and USFS MVUM layers before leaving cell range. Always enable GPS breadcrumb tracking so you can reverse your route in heavy fog or nightfall.
